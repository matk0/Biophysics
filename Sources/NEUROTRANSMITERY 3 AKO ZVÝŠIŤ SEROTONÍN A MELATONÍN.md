## Summary

The article presents serotonin and melatonin as a linked, context-dependent pathway beginning with tryptophan. B vitamins support its conversion, insulin can favour tryptophan uptake over competing amino acids, and seasonal temperature, food availability, and light influence whether tryptophan feeds serotonin or alternative pathways such as niacin and NAD. Serotonin is distributed differently between the gut and brain, so increasing it is not presented as universally desirable.

Darkness and circadian timing allow brain serotonin to be converted into melatonin. Vitamin D regulates the pathway differently through TPH2 in the brain and TPH1 in the gut, while the microbiome, sulfate, DHA, EPA, light, and darkness coordinate serotonin and melatonin production and use. EPA supports serotonin release, DHA supports its synthesis and light-dependent signalling, and melatonin protects and repairs mitochondria. Optimizing the pathway therefore depends on seasonal and circadian context rather than simply increasing tryptophan or carbohydrates.
